Treat eligibility as an **AND rule** in the spreadsheet: a pair qualifies only if it passes every check independently:

- Exactly two different crates, at most one of each.
- Total price **≤ $180**.
- Total kits **≥ 30**.
- Total mats **≥ 20**.
- **Both crates confirmed available by Friday**.

Keep kits and mats in separate columns. Extra kits cannot replace missing mats, and extra mats cannot replace missing kits. Likewise, sufficient quantities cannot make a tentative or late delivery eligible: the supplies must be confirmed for the required date.

| Pair | Price check | Kits check | Mats check | Friday check | Eligible now? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A + C** | $80 + $70 = **$150**, passes | 18 + 16 = **34**, passes | 10 + 8 = **18**, fails by 2 | Both confirmed | **No:** four extra kits do not cover two missing mats |
| **B + D** | $95 + $90 = **$185**, fails by $5 | 12 + 14 = **26**, fails by 4 | 15 + 12 = **27**, passes | D tentative | **No:** fails price, kits, and timing |
| **C + D** | $70 + $90 = **$160**, passes | 16 + 14 = **30**, passes exactly | 8 + 12 = **20**, passes exactly | D tentative | **No:** fails timing only |

If D becomes explicitly confirmed for Friday **before today’s order is placed**, C + D becomes eligible. B + D still fails because confirmation does not fix its price or kit shortage. A + C remains ineligible because it lacks two mats.

Compared with **A + B at $175**, **C + D at $160** saves **$15**, leaves **$20 rather than $5** in the budget, and supplies the same **30 kits**. You give up **five mats**: C + D supplies 20, versus A + B’s 25. Thus, the cheaper pair meets both quantity minimums exactly; A + B provides a five-mat cushion.

With supplier status unchanged, **A + B remains the only eligible pair**. Checking D is worthwhile, but tentative availability is not enough to approve C + D.
