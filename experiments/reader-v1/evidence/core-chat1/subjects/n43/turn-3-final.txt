Use the **4000 × 3000 original** for the display. It exceeds the required pixel density at 10 × 7.5 inches; the web copy falls below it.

The calculation is:

**Required pixels = print size in inches × required pixels per inch**

For a 10 × 7.5-inch print at 240 pixels per inch:

- Width: 10 × 240 = **2400 pixels**
- Height: 7.5 × 240 = **1800 pixels**

The minimum is therefore **2400 × 1800 pixels**.

To check an existing file, reverse the calculation:

**Pixels per inch = image pixels ÷ print size in inches**

| Copy | Pixel dimensions | Width calculation | Height calculation | Meets 240 PPI? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Original | 4000 × 3000 | 4000 ÷ 10 = **400 PPI** | 3000 ÷ 7.5 = **400 PPI** | Yes |
| Web | 1000 × 750 | 1000 ÷ 10 = **100 PPI** | 750 ÷ 7.5 = **100 PPI** | No |

Both files have the same 4:3 proportions as the print area, so neither needs cropping for this layout.

The three kinds of change have different effects:

- **Changing only the “dpi” label:** With resampling disabled, the pixel dimensions stay unchanged. Changing the web copy from 300 to 600 makes its nominal print size smaller—from about 3.33 × 2.5 inches to 1.67 × 1.25 inches. If you still print it at 10 × 7.5 inches, it still provides only 100 PPI.
- **Reducing the pixel dimensions, or downsampling:** This removes pixel data. That is what happened when the original became the 1000 × 750 web copy. It is useful for website exports, but the original should be preserved.
- **Enlarging the pixel dimensions, or upsampling:** Software calculates additional pixels from the existing ones. Enlarging the web copy to 2400 × 1800 would produce a nominal 240 PPI file, but it would not recover the original detail lost during downsampling.

Also distinguish resampling from simply changing the **physical print size**: printing the same pixels smaller raises their PPI; printing them larger lowers it.

A later **10 × 8-inch borderless layout** requires another decision. Its proportions are 5:4, while the photo is 4:3. You have these choices:

1. **Crop to fill the layout.** Using the original’s full 3000-pixel height, crop its width to **3750 pixels**. That removes 250 pixels across the width—125 from each side if centered. Choose the crop around the subject. The resulting 3750 × 3000 image prints at **375 PPI** at 10 × 8 inches, comfortably above the requirement. The minimum for this layout is **2400 × 1920 pixels**.
2. **Keep the whole photo and leave margins.** It fits at 10 × 7.5 inches, leaving half an inch of unused height, or a quarter-inch above and below when centered. This preserves the framing but does not provide borderless photographic coverage.
3. **Extend the background or design.** Keep the photo’s proportions and add content to fill the remaining area. Those additions are not part of the original photograph.

Stretching the photo directly to 10 × 8 would distort its shapes.

For each new job, establish the print dimensions, resolve any proportion mismatch, then calculate PPI using the pixels remaining after cropping. These calculations check the production requirement; inspecting the image or a sample print is still needed to assess its finished appearance.
