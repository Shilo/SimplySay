**“300 dpi” doesn’t mean the two files contain equal detail.** In a photo file, that label usually means a suggested print density—more accurately, 300 pixels per inch (ppi). Pixel dimensions and compression matter more for sharpness.

- **Community display prints:** use the original with the most pixels and least compression. Divide its width and height in pixels by 300 to find its print size at 300 ppi. For example, 3000 × 2400 pixels gives 10 × 8 inches.
- **Website:** use an optimized copy with enough pixels for its displayed size. A photo displayed 800 pixels wide typically needs a 1600-pixel-wide image for sharpness on high-density screens. If the web copy looks soft, export a larger or less compressed version from the original.

Changing the dpi label alone won’t restore lost detail.
