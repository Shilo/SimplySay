**“300 dpi” alone doesn’t tell you how sharp a photo is.** It usually records the intended print density—more accurately, 300 pixels per inch (PPI). Pixel dimensions determine how much detail is available: a 3000-pixel-wide photo prints 10 inches wide at 300 PPI. Changing the label alone adds no detail.

The web copy may look softer because it has fewer pixels, stronger JPEG compression, or is being enlarged on screen. Both copies can still carry the same “300 dpi” label.

Generally:

- **Printed community display:** use the full-resolution original. Divide its pixel dimensions by the intended print dimensions in inches to find the actual PPI.
- **Website:** use a suitably sized, compressed copy made from the original. Choose enough pixels for its displayed size—often twice the displayed width for high-density screens. Browsers generally ignore the DPI label.

If the current web copy looks soft at its intended display size, export a better web version from the original.
