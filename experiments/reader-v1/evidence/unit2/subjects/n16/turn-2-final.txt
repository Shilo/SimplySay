**Offline green means all checks passed using that phone’s clock and stored information. It does not establish current permission to enter or prevent reuse on another offline phone.** Here is the reasoning from [scanner-notes.md](<TASK_ROOT>/scanner-notes.md).

The QR’s `kid` selects a bundled Ed25519 public key. Successful signature verification proves that the trusted issuer signed the event, ticket ID, and validity-window fields, and that those signed fields have not changed.

That verification does not contact a server. It therefore cannot establish whether the ticket was subsequently canceled or used. Copying an authentic QR also preserves its valid signature.

For offline green, four conditions must all hold:

- The signature verifies.
- The phone’s clock falls within the signed validity window.
- The ticket ID is absent from the phone’s last imported cancellation snapshot.
- The ticket ID is absent from that phone’s local used-ticket set.

**T-41 passes on both offline phones, despite being canceled.**

Both cancellation snapshots date from 09:00. The server canceled T-41 at 09:10, so neither snapshot contains that cancellation. Cancellation does not change the already-issued QR or invalidate its signature.

At actual 10:00, the scans proceed as follows:

| Check | Phone A, scanned first | Phone B, scanned afterward |
|---|---|---|
| Signature | Valid | Valid |
| Phone time within 09:00–13:00 | Yes: 10:00 | Yes: 09:58 |
| Absent from stored cancellation snapshot | Yes | Yes |
| Absent from this phone’s used-ticket set | Yes | Yes |
| Result | Accepts; records T-41 locally | Accepts; records T-41 locally |

A’s acceptance changes only A’s used-ticket set. B receives no update while offline, so its duplicate check still passes. Afterward, another scan on either phone would be rejected by that phone’s local duplicate check.

There are two independent gaps here: stale cancellation information allows a canceled ticket, and separate used-ticket sets allow duplicate admission. Either problem can occur without the other. The prototype shows no separate freshness warning alongside green.

**T-99 produces different results because the phones disagree about time.**

For each phone’s first scan, the signature is valid, the ticket is not canceled, and its ID is absent from the local used-ticket set. That leaves the validity-window check:

| At actual 10:00 | Phone clock | Comparison with expiry at 09:59 | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phone A | 10:00 | Already expired | Rejects |
| Phone B | 09:58 | Still within the window | Accepts; records T-99 locally |

The signature correctly protects the stated expiry time. The error comes from comparing that authentic expiry against B’s slow clock. A’s rejection does not record an admission or affect B.

**With a working server connection, subsequent scans use server time, current cancellations, and the shared admission ledger.**

At or after 10:00, the online path rejects T-41 because it is canceled and T-99 because it has expired. B’s slow clock no longer determines the time check.

For an otherwise allowed, unused ticket, the server checks and records use as one indivisible operation—an *atomic check-and-consume*. Two competing online scans cannot both claim the unused ticket: one succeeds, and the other is rejected.

Reconnection also uploads earlier offline admissions for audit. It cannot undo entry or retroactively prevent the two T-41 admissions. The notes do not specify how those uploaded records are reconciled with the shared ledger, how conflicts are handled, or how long reconnection takes; those behaviors remain unspecified in this prototype.
