## Why a new full-goal mechanism is not justified here

The final-answer clause is unchanged across concrete.v1–v4. Successive progress edits therefore cannot diagnose or improve its causal contribution. Nevertheless, the final ties are not all the same kind of evidence:

- A's source already explains shared-folder synchronization versus retained backup plainly. Its untreated replies give the distinction, uncertainty and practical checks understandably. This offers little demonstrated headroom for the concrete-language mechanism.
- C supplies genuinely technical scanner notes. Its untreated first reply still translates the checks into practical meaning and works through stale cancellations, separate phone records and the wrong clock. Its matched enabled reply makes largely the same explanation. Thus the ceiling concern survived one deliberate change in source difficulty; it is not explained solely by plain source prose.
- W1 expressly asks to keep routine detail in files and chat on the decision. Its second turn asks for only the claim decision and present choice. These are useful preservation and progress tests, but weak tests of unconstrained ordinary final-answer effort.
- C's maintainer turn and the repair/handoff requests deliberately require causal depth and examples. They test adaptive detail and preservation. Their substantial necessary content is not an unsuitable response to those requests.
- The simple time questions are satisfactory guardrails. They cannot establish a general explanation or jargon gain.

These are limits of the sampled opportunities, not permission to manufacture a verbose baseline. The saved examples do not establish a recurring final-answer defect that a different mechanism could specifically repair.

I also checked the historical A/B candidate rationale. Relevance to the current question, stopping once it is satisfied, supplying changed information in follow-ups and adding missing understanding were already tested. Recasting those as “answer the reader's open question,” “each sentence must earn its place” or “report only a useful delta” would change the vocabulary rather than the mechanism. A post-draft relevance audit would likewise need evidence of a distinct operation; calling the same content gate an editing pass is insufficient. General restructuring or tighter claim/evidence grouping might be useful, but these records do not identify a scattered-argument failure or predict a specific repair. I would not spend a fresh comparison on that speculation.

## Strongest objection and a falsifiable reconsideration rule

The strongest objection is that the final-answer mechanism has never been revised, and the cases only sparsely sample ordinary explanations or recommendations without an additional brevity instruction. A structurally different mechanism could help such tasks. The historical ties cannot establish that it would fail, and my recommendation is a stop on the present rationale, not a product-impossibility claim. N33's independent task audit can contradict this diagnosis.

The **stop recommendation is overturned** if root's reconciliation identifies all three of the following before dispatch:

1. A realistic ordinary-chat problem with a request-derived account of the reader's avoidable effort. Specify the missing connection, misleading arrangement or other concrete burden; length, source complexity or a hypothetical taste for shorter answers is insufficient. Preserve already-satisfactory baseline evidence and do not select among repeated baseline runs for a weak answer.
2. One exact compact mechanism that predicts an observable change beyond current-question relevance, incremental follow-ups, concrete terms/causes or required-stage fallback. Explain why the existing rules would not already predict that change. It must cover ordinary finals and progress while leaving full work, requested detail and protected surfaces intact.
3. A prospective loss condition: on the first complete matched pair, an optional repeated work inventory, deferred necessary warning/decision, lost final reasoning, or no predicted structural change defeats that mechanism's diagnostic claim. Satisfactory final ties remain ties; a progress preference cannot substitute for a final-answer benefit. Any usefulness claim still needs the existing independent per-goal assessment and actual-work checks.

That is a concrete route to changing this recommendation without automatically spending the available allocation. Until it is met, more synonyms, stage exceptions or unchanged W1 responses would test persistence of an unresolved pattern rather than a new explanation for it.
