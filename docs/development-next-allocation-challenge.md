## Clear ties and the original final rule

There is a real design tension, but the original three-of-four rule is not automatically logically inconsistent with allowing a clear response to stay unchanged. A correct tie can be a good response and still supply no evidence for a broad efficacy claim. Requiring at least one improvement on each case measures breadth across both cases. If one case is already clear in both repetitions, that rule correctly withholds the broader qualification even if another case improves.

If the owner's intended claim is instead conditional benefit where a real burden exists plus unchanged clear replies elsewhere, that is a different estimand and needs an explicitly approved, predeclared rule before final subjects or unsealing. Retain the original benchmark, all four matched pairs and their denominator as history. Do not retrospectively discard clear cases, invent an opportunity after seeing enabled output, or convert an undefined “concrete benefit” into a pass. Specify opportunity classification, required repeatability, agreement, ties, disagreements, missing evidence and preservation failures for any new narrower claim. A narrower result must remain narrower in the final recommendation.

Recommended treatment of the current proposal: retain the old broad qualification benchmark; resolve any additional burden-conditional claim prospectively before opening the sealed cases. It is acceptable for final evidence to be informative without earning broad qualification. No final gate has been run or failed yet; the present stop is the failed development gate.
